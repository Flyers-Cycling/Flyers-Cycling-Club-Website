Flyers coaches use an emergency action plan for each place we ride. NICA describes the three parts of a good plan as emergency response info, weather and environmental considerations, and trail and terrain assessments. Ours follow the same shape.

## Emergency response

- The nearest hospital or urgent care, with the address and drive time.
- Where an ambulance can reach the trail, and the address or coordinates to give 911.
- Where cell service works and where it doesn't.
- Who calls 911, who stays with the injured rider, and who looks after the rest of the group.
- How we reach parents.

## Weather and environment

- Where to take shelter if lightning moves in. Lightning can come up mid-ride, so every route needs a plan.
- Heat, cold, and how far the group is from the cars.
- When we call off a ride (lightning and mud), and that we post it in Slack at least 2 hours before.

## Trails and terrain

- The riskiest trails and sections, and areas to stay out of.
- Bail-out routes back to the trailhead.
- Where groups regroup.

## Using the plan

Read the plan for a location before you lead a ride there. Keep a copy on your phone or in your pack. If something on the trail changes, like a washout or a closed section, tell the head coach so the plan gets updated.
